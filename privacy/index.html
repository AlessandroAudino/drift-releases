Privacy policy

Drift is made by Alessandro Audino, an independent developer in Italy. This page explains in plain words what the Drift app and this website do with information about you. The short version: Drift doesn't collect anything about you.

The Drift app

What it looks at. To let weather settle on your windows, Drift reads where the windows on your screen are and how big they are. It never reads window titles or what's inside windows, and never records or captures your screen. These positions stay on your computer and are forgotten as soon as they've been used.

What it stores. Your settings (the weather you picked, your preferences, the city whose sunrise and sunset the wallpapers follow, and your previous wallpaper so it can be brought back) are saved only on your computer. Until you pick a city, Drift guesses it from your computer's time zone. It works out sunrise and sunset by itself, without going online. If you write a wish for the new year, it stays there too, and so does a small tally of your year with Drift (days, weathers, gifts opened) for the summary it shows on New Year's Eve.

What it sends. Nothing about you. Drift has no account, no analytics, no ads and no crash reports.

Updates. About a minute after it starts, and then every six hours, Drift downloads a small file from its public download page on GitHub to see whether there's a new version, and if there is, downloads it from there. As with any visit to a website, GitHub receives your computer's internet (IP) address and basic technical details; GitHub handles these under its own privacy statement. You can turn automatic updates off in Settings › General › Updates.

Invite codes. If you were given a personal invite code, Drift keeps it on your computer and uses it there to open what is locked for that code: test versions, or messages meant for you. Both come from the same download page; with a code for messages, Drift looks there every quarter of an hour. The code itself, and what it opens, are never sent anywhere.

Permissions on a Mac. If you turn on snow on the Dock, macOS asks you to allow Drift under Accessibility and to let it control “System Events”: Drift uses this only to find where the Dock is. Setting the wallpaper can occasionally ask for the “System Events” permission too, and uses it for nothing else.

This website

No cookies, no tracking. This website uses no cookies, no analytics and no advertising, and loads nothing from other companies (no outside fonts either), apart from GitHub, which hosts it.

Hosting. The website and the downloads are hosted by GitHub (GitHub, Inc.). When you visit, GitHub receives your IP address and the usual technical details your browser sends, to deliver the pages and keep the service secure. See GitHub's privacy statement.

Download page. To show the newest version, the download page asks GitHub for a small file that names it.

Your choices. If you pick a language or a weather, your browser remembers it on your device (in its local storage). It's never sent anywhere.

If you write to me

If you email email address coming soon, your message and address are used only to answer you, and deleted when they're no longer needed.

Your rights

Under the EU's data protection rules (GDPR) you can ask what information about you is held, and have it corrected or deleted. Because Drift and this website don't collect personal information, there's usually nothing to hand over, but you can always ask at email address coming soon. You can also complain to the Italian data protection authority, the Garante per la protezione dei dati personali.

Changes

If Drift ever adds a feature that sends information (for example, real weather for your city), it will stay off until you turn it on, and this page will be updated first.
